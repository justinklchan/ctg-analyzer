Horizon

Minutes before the end of the trace. The score is read at the latest window at least this far out.

Attributes

A filled dot marks a gap whose interval clears zero at the horizon shown; a hollow one, at a different horizon only. Alarm-rate gaps do not count — those are supposed to differ.

Does the risk score work equally well for everyone?

A subgroup audit of the out-of-fold risk score, in the fairlearn sense: one shared operating point, and error rates measured separately inside each group. Nothing is refit here — this reads the same out-of-fold scores the record explorer draws.

Two questions that both get called “fairness”

They pull in opposite directions on this cohort, so it is worth separating them before reading any number below.

Does it alarm equally often?

Demographic parity. Same share of each group flagged. On this cohort that is the wrong target: nulliparous labours really do end in acidaemia about twice as often as parous ones, and forcing the alarm rates level would mean deliberately under-warning the higher-risk group. A gap in the alarm rate here is expected, and on its own it is not a finding.

Does it catch equally well?

Equal opportunity. Among the labours that were acidaemic, the same share detected. Different base rates do not excuse a gap here: a missed case is a missed case, and two women with the same outcome should have had the same chance of being warned. This is the metric to read first.

Both are measured at a single cohort-wide threshold — Youden’s J on the log-odds at delivery, the same operating point the record explorer uses. That is deliberate: a deployed alarm has one threshold, and choosing a separate cut per group is a mitigation, not a measurement. It would equalise the very thing being measured here.

What this audit cannot tell you

The usual axes are not in the data. CTU-CHB is one hospital in Brno over 2010–2012, and its headers carry no race, ethnicity, language, insurance or deprivation field. Every grouping on this page is a clinical or device variable. Nothing here speaks to the disparities those absent fields would be needed to see, and their absence is not evidence that there are none.

The intervals are narrower than the real uncertainty. They are a bootstrap over records, so they cover which labours landed in the evaluation set — and nothing else. They do not cover the model’s own seed-to-seed instability, which on this cohort moves AUROC at delivery over 0.659–0.706. A gap whose interval clears zero here has passed the easier of the two tests; confirming it needs the refit band from risk_uncertainty.py, run per subgroup.

Small groups are not scored. A group with fewer than 10 acidaemic or ten non-acidaemic records is shown with its counts and left blank for the rates. Pre-eclampsia has 17 records and 4 of them acidaemic; there is no honest sensitivity to report from four events, so none is.

A gap is not yet a cause. None of these attributes was randomly assigned. Where an attribute is also a model input it is marked, because that changes what the gap means — but even the axes the model never sees carry whatever else differs about those labours.
